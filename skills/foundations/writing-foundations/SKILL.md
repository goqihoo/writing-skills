# Writing Foundations

Make the document give its reader a usable capability: understand, decide, act, verify, or find an answer.

## Workflow

1. **Set the outcome.** Identify the primary reader, the question the document resolves, and what the reader should be able to do afterward. Keep one primary task; do not require a fixed goal statement in the document.
2. **Respect the artifact.** Follow the owning deliverable skill, repository instructions, and healthy local conventions. Preserve verified facts, links, decisions, and scope unless the user asks to change them.
3. **Build the reader path.** List the questions the reader must answer in order. Make each answer supply the context needed by the next question.
4. **Write claims and support.** Give each section one controlling point. Separate observed facts, interpretations, and choices. Connect claims to sources, reasoning, or concrete examples.
5. **Expose uncertainty.** Mark missing evidence, unresolved decisions, assumptions, and disputed claims. Request input only when a missing decision would materially change the artifact; otherwise proceed with an explicit assumption.
6. **Write boundaries.** State prerequisites, non-goals, exceptions, failure modes, and conditions that would change the conclusion.
7. **Make language concrete.** Name the actor, action, condition, and observable result. When a key term first appears, explain what it is, why it matters, and one example. Put a representative end-to-end case before formulas, dimensions, or layers.
8. **Compress for use.** Remove material that does not change understanding, action, judgment, or lookup. Use prose for reasoning, tables for repeated fields, and diagrams only when relationships are hard to follow linearly.
9. **Verify.** Test the document against its intended reader action and the owning skill's completion criteria.

## Shared completion check

- The document resolves one primary reader task.
- Headings follow the reader's questions rather than the order in which material was collected.
- Facts, interpretations, choices, assumptions, and open questions remain distinguishable.
- Abstract terms resolve to concrete objects, actions, conditions, or examples.
- Important conclusions state their basis and boundary.
- The document contains no unsupported agreement, invented fact, or hidden material decision.
- A reader can recognize when the document is complete and when it needs revision.
