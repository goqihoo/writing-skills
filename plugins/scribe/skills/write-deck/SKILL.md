# Write Deck

Make one slide sequence help its audience understand, believe, decide, learn, or act.

## Workflow

1. Identify the audience, setting, delivery mode, available time or page budget, and result the presentation must produce.
2. Read the target, verified sources, repository instructions, and healthy local examples. Preserve facts, links, decisions, scope, terminology, and binding visual conventions.
3. State the communication job in one sentence: by the end, this audience should reach this outcome because they understand or accept this central point.
4. Read `references/deck-writing-method.md`. Derive a cumulative narrative path from the communication job and source material; treat business, product, technical, learning, proposal, and other settings as content and context rather than fixed deck types.
5. Lock the deck plan: opening, reasoning path, section responsibilities, evidence, examples, ending, and the unique job of every slide. Check the title sequence and every adjacent transition before drafting slide bodies.
6. Read `../../methods/prose-quality.md` and adapt `assets/deck-manuscript-template.md` as the production scaffold. Keep only fields that help the actual delivery, and do not expose planning labels as audience-facing copy. Draft titles and screen copy, speaker support, transitions, and source notes within the locked plan. Keep exact code, commands, formulas, schemas, quotations, and technical labels unchanged.
7. Read `../../methods/visual-production.md` when the request includes a visual or the deck would otherwise force the audience to reconstruct a central relationship. Create a supporting visual inside this workflow when warranted; keep the presentation manuscript in Markdown.
8. Recheck the result against the communication job, source authority, delivery mode, time or page budget, local conventions, and any requested shorter version.

## Boundaries

- Own one audience-facing presentation manuscript in Markdown, including its narrative path and slide-level content.
- Support business, product, technical, learning, planning, and mixed professional scenarios without forcing them into registered deck types.
- Treat the deck as a derived expression. Preserve and link the sources that own company facts, product commitments, technical truth, project state, evidence, and decisions.
- Produce Markdown and supporting assets only. Do not create PowerPoint, Keynote, or PDF presentation files in this version.
- Let another artifact skill own a requested authoritative document whose primary result is not a presentation manuscript.

## Completion

The work is complete when the audience and intended outcome are explicit, the slide sequence forms one cumulative argument or learning path, every slide has a distinct job, titles and transitions expose the reasoning, screen copy and speaker support sound natural in their delivery setting, consequential claims remain traceable, and the Markdown manuscript resolves the requested presentation task without pretending to be an authoritative source or a rendered presentation file.
